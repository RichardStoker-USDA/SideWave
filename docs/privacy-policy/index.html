Privacy Policy

SideWave processes dictation and meeting recordings on your Mac. It stores local history and recovery data. Optional delivery to other apps and voice-agent clients has a separate privacy boundary.

Local Processing and Storage

  • Dictation audio: Normal dictation processes microphone audio in memory. Finalized and provisional text can be written to recovery files. Recovery files are cleared after their text is saved to history, or after an explicit discard.
  • Meeting audio: Audio is written to ~/Library/Application Support/SideWave/Meetings/InProgress/ during recording. Session folders use owner-only permissions. An interrupted meeting can be recovered from these files. Audio for a complete, saved transcript can remain while results are open for speaker reprocessing, then is removed when dismissed or cleaned up on a later launch. Partial transcripts and failed history saves retain their source audio. Unfinished audio remains until you finish processing or explicitly discard it, regardless of history retention.
  • History: Dictation text and target-app metadata are stored in history.json; meeting transcripts and notes are stored in meeting-history.json under SideWave's Application Support folder. Available earlier recognition and refine stages can be retained with an entry.
  • Vocabulary and speech profile: Custom glossaries, aliases, usage notes, and an optional speech profile stay in local SideWave storage. No glossary packs are bundled by default; optional bundles are downloaded on request, with manual updates by default and per-bundle automatic checks only after explicit opt-in. Local models can process text you provide to vocabulary assistance or the speech-profile builder.
  • Document drafts: The current draft for each template and the documents in Recents are stored in document_drafts.json. A cancelled model edit restores the prior document. Clear document removes the active draft's content. Recents and History are separate.
  • Settings: Preferences, including hotkeys, microphones, templates, output language, and retention, are stored in UserDefaults.
  • Models: Speech and language weights are cached locally. The model-storage view shows app-managed caches and removal actions. Apple Intelligence assets are managed by macOS and require its feature to be enabled and ready.

Storage errors are shown in the app. An unreadable history, document-draft, network-history, or vocabulary file is preserved rather than overwritten. Failed writes may leave recovery files longer than a normal successful session. Copy important text somewhere safe before quitting if a save error remains.

Refine and Meeting Formatting

Recognition uses CoreML. Refine, meeting formatting, vocabulary assistance, and speech-profile generation use Apple Intelligence or a local Gemma 4 model through MLX. These processing steps do not send their prompts to a SideWave cloud inference service. After the required assets are available, local inference can run offline.

Generated text can be inaccurate. Review output before sending it elsewhere.

Other Apps and Voice-Agent Clients

Pasting into a browser, chat app, document service, or terminal sends text to that application. Its storage and network behavior are outside SideWave's control.

The optional MCP integration exposes a Unix socket to processes running under your macOS account. Connected clients can read the live transcript and submit speech or draft responses. A client may forward transcript text to a remote model or service. Keep MCP disabled when it is not needed, and connect only clients you intend to trust.

Owner-only file permissions protect against other local users. They do not prevent software running under your own account from accessing the same files or socket.

Network Activity

  • Model preparation: SideWave fetches manifests and model files from Hugging Face and associated download hosts. The selected models and enabled features determine the size. Existing files are reused when valid; interrupted downloads may need repair.
  • Glossary catalog: Opening the optional catalog fetches its index from GitHub. Downloading a bundle fetches the selected JSON file and checks its size, checksum, schema, and term count. Updates are manual by default. Enabling automatic updates permits a daily catalog check and changed bundle downloads while SideWave is open. Search runs locally over the fetched index. Personal vocabulary is never uploaded.
  • Updates: Sparkle fetches the release feed from sidewave.dev and downloads releases from GitHub. SideWave asks once before checking automatically, and you can change that in Settings > About. Sparkle's system-profile submission is off.
  • Links: Opening online help, license pages, or project links uses your browser and those sites' policies.

Remote hosts receive ordinary request metadata, including your network address. SideWave has no built-in analytics or automatic crash-report upload service. The Data Transparency window retains app-managed requests and downloads until you clear the log, including failures. It stores host, path, byte count, and time, without request bodies, query strings, credentials, or transcript text. It is not a complete system network audit.

Retention and Deletion

Dictation history defaults to seven days and is capped at 500 entries. Retention is applied on launch and after dictation. Settings offers other periods or Keep Forever. Individual entries and all history can be deleted from the app. Export or copy important results before deleting them.

Unfinished meeting recordings remain on this Mac until you finish processing or explicitly discard them, regardless of history retention. Completed audio and discarded meetings have separate cleanup actions described above. Reset options distinguish restoring preferences from removing saved data; read the confirmation before choosing a full reset.

Microphone and Accessibility Permissions

SideWave explains permissions before requesting them. Microphone access is used for dictation, meetings, microphone tests, and enabled audio diagnostics. An optional standby setting can keep the input device open between dictations; disable it if you want the input closed while idle.

Accessibility is used for the global hotkey, target-window and text-field selection, and text delivery. Denying a permission prevents the corresponding operation and shows a recovery action. You can revoke either permission in System Settings > Privacy & Security.
